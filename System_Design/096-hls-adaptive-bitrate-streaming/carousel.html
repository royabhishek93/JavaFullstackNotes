Keyframe Alignment

Why Switching Only Happens at Segment Boundaries

Every rendition's keyframes land at the EXACT same timestamps (every 2-6s).

Can't cleanly jump from a keyframe in one bitrate to a mid-group frame in another — decoder needs an aligned boundary.

Throughput-Based ABR

Signal 1

Pick the highest rendition whose bitrate fits within ~85% of recently measured download speed.

✕ Can oscillate on bursty networks — 1 fast segment triggers upswitch, 1 slow one triggers downswitch.

Buffer-Based ABR

Signal 2

Deep buffer (>20s) → be aggressive even on borderline throughput.

Shallow buffer (<10s) → force downgrade immediately, regardless of throughput reading.

The Rebuffer Trap

Bandwidth Drop Timeline

Buffer drains from 24s → 0.5s FASTER than the ABR downswitch takes effect → real rebuffer event, spinner shown.

Fix: buffer-based logic acts earlier than pure throughput logic alone would.

FFmpeg: Forcing Alignment

Encoding Side

-g 180 -keyint_min 180 -sc_threshold 0 -hls_time 6

GOP size (180 frames @ 30fps = 6s) must exactly match segment duration. Disables scene-change keyframes that would break alignment.

"The player continuously measures throughput and buffer occupancy. A pure throughput-based approach can oscillate on bursty networks, so combine it with buffer occupancy: if the buffer drops below a low watermark, force a downgrade immediately regardless of what the throughput estimate says."
