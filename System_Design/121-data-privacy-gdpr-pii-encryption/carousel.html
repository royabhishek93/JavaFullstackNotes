Scenario — Tokenization

One vault. Every else gets a token.

Checkout Service
4111-1111-1111-1111
→
VAULT
real card ↔ token
→
tok_9f8a3b2c only

Breach the analytics warehouse → attacker gets tokens, not card numbers. Delete one vault row → every token everywhere is instantly meaningless.

TRAP: storing the raw card number in "a column with an encryption flag" pulls your ENTIRE app into PCI-DSS scope.
Slide 3 of 10
Scenario — GDPR Erasure Saga

Deletion is an orchestrated workflow.

Deletion Orchestrator (Saga)
↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
DB: delete + tombstone
ES: delete doc
Redis: invalidate
S3: delete files
Backup: pending-purge list
TRAP: backups can't be edited in place — they're tracked and purged only on natural retention expiry. And every step must land in an audit log — GDPR requires proof, not good faith.

Scenario — Two Different Threats

In transit vs. at rest.

→ TLS 1.3 →
Protects against a network eavesdropper mid-wire
AES-256 disk / column
Protects against a stolen disk or public bucket
TRAP: having one does NOT imply the other. Verify both layers explicitly — they stop completely different attackers.

Real Numbers

What this costs in production.

<5ms

Tokenization vault lookup — added only at actual card-charge time, not on every token reference.

30d

GDPR erasure SLA — "without undue delay," commonly a 30-day max — why deletion is a saga, not a sync call.

<1ms

AES-256 field encrypt/decrypt CPU cost — the KMS DEK unwrap round-trip (a few ms) dominates, so DEKs get cached.

Slide 9 of 10
Interview Cheat Sheet

Decision tree + the exact answer.

Is it card/gov-ID data? → Tokenization vault, isolate compliance scope.
Is it health/precise-location? → Field-level encryption + strict access logging.
Is it name/email/address? → Encrypt at rest + deletion-saga path.
Is it in transit? → TLS everywhere, including service-to-service mTLS.
Deleting a user? → Orchestrated saga, not a single DELETE.

"The first architectural decision is to never let the raw card number propagate beyond the minimum system that legally needs it... rotating the master key later doesn't require re-encrypting the entire dataset."
