Video Transcoding Pipeline

Slide 1 / 9 — The Hook

2+ hours → <15 min

Sequential transcoding of a 2-hour video's highest rung alone takes over 2 hours. Parallelized correctly: under 15 minutes.

Sequential Transcoding Is Slow

The Problem

1 worker

One worker runs all 5 rungs one after another — ~9.6 min even for a 10-min video, dominated by the slowest (1080p) rung.

Parallel-By-Rung

First Axis

1080p
720p
480p
360p
240p

Each rung on its own worker, concurrently — 9.6 min drops to ~4 min.

Time-Chunking

Second Axis — The Real Unlock

Split a 2-hour source into 10 keyframe-aligned 12-min chunks (stream-copy, near-instant). Dispatch each chunk × each rung = up to 50 concurrent jobs.

Concatenate (stream-copy) back into 5 final renditions once all chunks finish.

Keyframe Alignment

Required for Adaptive Streaming

-g 48 -keyint_min 48 -sc_threshold 0

Every rung gets a keyframe at the SAME timestamp — the player needs this to switch bitrates cleanly later.

The Worker Crash Trap

Where Corruption Sneaks In

Spot-instance worker killed mid-encode → truncated file still "EXISTS" on disk.

✕ Checking file presence alone silently ships a corrupt chunk. ✓ Validate checksum/duration instead.

Idempotent Jobs + Dead-Letter Queue

Reliability

Job key = videoId + rendition + chunkIndex — redelivery never creates conflicting output.

Max retries exceeded → dead-letter queue + alert, not infinite retry.

Decision Tree & Interview Answer

Quick Reference

Long video, tight publish SLA? → Parallel-by-rung + time-chunking. Rarely-watched archive content? → On-demand transcode instead.

"The design has to parallelize on two axes: across rungs, and within a single rung across time. Keyframes must be aligned identically across every rung — the player needs to switch bitrates cleanly at segment boundaries."
